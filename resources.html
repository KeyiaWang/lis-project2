Implicit Bias: A Starter Guide

New to these terms? Start here. This guide connects core concepts from LIS 500 to everyday technology and real-world examples.

What is implicit bias?

Implicit Bias is an unconscious and unintentional belief or thought about a specific group of people that changes and influences the way that a person behaves towards that specific group. An example of this is something like resume screening, where two identical resumes receive much different treatment, because one has a traditional white name, and another has a traditional muslim name.

Why it matters in tech:

When automated hiring tools or screening algorithms are trained on historical human decisions, they end up learning and repeating these exact unconscious biases at scale.

The Implicit Association Test (IAT)

I thought that the IAT test from Harvard was very interesting, and unlike anything else I have ever done before. Before taking the test, I was curious to see how something could test implicit bias, and I think that the IAT test does as good a job as possible to test for something that is unconscious. However, I think it is impossible to truly understand implicit bias inside of a person, as it is something that is unconscious, and not on purpose.

Try it yourself:

Testing your own reaction times helps make an invisible concept visible, showing how societal stereotypes influence subconscious processing.

Intersectionality

Intersectionality is the idea that people's race, gender, and other such factors that define their identity, impact and shape their experiences in the world. Further, it is the idea that a single person's experience cannot be understood by looking at only one part of their identity, you must look at all parts.

Why it matters in tech:

In tech spaces, addressing gender alone often overlooks the unique barriers faced by women of color. Looking at technology through an intersectional lens explains why initiatives like Black Girls Code (featured on our Tech Heroes page) are critical for addressing compounding inequalities.
